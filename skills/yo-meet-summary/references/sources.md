# Research and decisions — yo-meet-summary

This skill is grounded first in the owner's real working expectations, then in
external meeting guidance. External practices supplement the workflow; they do
not override the supplied meeting context or turn one example into a universal
template.

Authority labels:

- **O:** owner-supplied workflow and real-use feedback
- **I:** official organizational or product guidance

## Owner-supplied source of truth

The owner supplied direct guidance from management and sales leadership on
2026-08-20, then iterated the output against a real weekly product meeting.
Those inputs established these rules:

- Know whether the meeting is for updates, open discussion, or a mix; updates
  come first in a mixed meeting.
- Know the subject and the participant's role. Ask the host when either is
  unclear.
- Use a brief when live participants need more context, and keep presentation
  slides sparse enough to understand quickly.
- Start with the meeting goal, communicate clearly, contribute ideas, challenge
  constructively, take ownership, and flag missed items.
- Send notes after every work meeting with a concise summary, explicit
  decisions, actionable work, owners, and dates in the appropriate channel.
- For weekly product work, the proven readable shape is a quick summary,
  optional workstream updates, real agreements, next steps, optional follow-up,
  and optional `cc` for visibility.
- The preferred voice is concise, easy to understand, and professional but
  slightly less formal than standard corporate writing.

The real company notes and personal or company names used during discovery are
not copied into this public reference.

## External evidence

- **I — Atlassian, Page-Led Meetings:** purpose, scope, desired outcomes, and key
  discussion points should be visible up front; written context is most useful
  for decision or consensus work; durable notes should capture the decision and
  why it was made. This supports conditional pre-reads, outcome-first framing,
  and decision rationale without requiring a page for every meeting.
  <https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/page-led-meeting>

- **I — GitLab, Live Doc Meetings:** agendas carry goals, pre-work, intent, and
  expected outcomes; questions prepared in advance improve the conversation;
  notes need not be verbatim but should preserve key points, decisions, and next
  steps. This supports concise notes and a shared artifact without adopting
  GitLab's exact timing or formatting rules.
  <https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/live-doc-meetings/>

- **I — Microsoft WorkLab, Hybrid Meetings:** meeting types require different
  preparation; discuss-and-decide meetings benefit from a structured agenda, a
  proposal people can react to, a known decision process, and a record of
  decisions, responsibilities, and actions. This supports intent-based output
  shapes rather than one universal template.
  <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/guides/how-to-get-hybrid-meetings-right>

- **I — Google Meet Help, Take Notes for Me:** the product separates summary,
  decision states, next steps, and optional detail, while warning that generated
  summaries can be incomplete or inaccurate. This supports distinct internal
  classifications, concise-by-default output, and an explicit no-invention
  boundary; the skill does not inherit Google's exact UI schema.
  <https://support.google.com/meet/answer/14754931?hl=en>

- **I — Microsoft Writing Style Guide:** concise sentences, natural technical
  language, consistent terminology, and strong information order improve
  scannability. This supports the professional-but-natural voice.
  <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/contribute/content/style-quick-start>

## Bundle decisions

### Kept

- Exactly two stages: Before Meeting and After Meeting.
- Product and business outcome first, with technical fluency retained where it
  changes the decision or execution.
- Adaptive formats for updates, decisions, discussions, and mixed meetings.
- Explicit evidence, decision, action, ownership, date, follow-up, and mention
  rules.
- Draft-only behavior unless the user separately authorizes external delivery.

### Deliberately not adopted

- A mandatory 24- or 72-hour pre-read window.
- A full DACI/RACI process for ordinary meetings.
- Verbatim transcripts, forced recordings, or speaker-by-speaker minutes.
- One fixed template for every meeting type.
- Mandatory `Still to confirm`, empty sections, duplicated follow-up, or guessed
  Slack handles.
- Customer-facing publication or automatic channel posting by default.
